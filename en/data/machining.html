📉 Taylor's Tool Life Equation — Tool Life vs. Speed
Vc × Tⁿ = C — n depends on the tool/material pair (HSS≈0.1-0.15, Carbide≈0.2-0.4, Ceramic≈0.5-0.7). The larger n is, the more the tool "forgives" a speed increase.
Rule of thumb: raising Vc by 20% shortens tool life by about 50% (for n≈0.25, typical for carbide). Before speeding up production — check the real cost in tool wear!

🌀 Chip Thinning — Feed Compensation in Partial Side Milling
When ae (radial depth) is less than 50% of the tool diameter, the actual chip thickness is smaller than the nominal fz — you can (and should!) increase fz to maintain proper tooth loading and prevent rubbing.
Approximate formula: fz_corrected = fz × D/√(D×ae−ae²). At ae=10%D, you can usually multiply fz by 1.5-2×!

🚿 Coolant Delivery Methods
Flood
The most common. Cheap, reliable. Doesn't always reach the cutting zone in depth.
Through-Tool
20-70bar through holes in the tool. Mandatory for deep drilling (L/D>5). Extends tool life significantly.
MQL (Minimum Quantity Lubrication)
Oil mist 5-50ml/h. Green and economical. Excellent for aluminum, less so for deep drilling.
Dry Cutting
Requires TiAlN/AlCrN coating. Cast iron, high-speed steel milling. Saves 15% in operating costs.
🧪 Coolant Concentration — Testing and Maintenance
Emulsion concentration: 5-10% measured with a refractometer. Desired pH: 8.5-9.5. Bacteria = bad odor + skin conditions — weekly testing!
Aluminum (6061/7075)
Vc: 200-500 (carbide)
fz: 0.10-0.25mm
Coolant: dry/light emulsion
Tip: BUE is the main issue at low Vc — raise speed, sharp tool, DLC coating. Wide chip-clearance flutes.
Mild/Medium Steel
Vc: 60-120 (carbide)
fz: 0.08-0.20mm
Coolant: emulsion 8-10%
Tip: The default starting point. P-Grade insert, TiAlN. Moving to 4140 Q&T — reduce Vc by 30-40%.
Stainless (304/316)
Vc: 40-70 (carbide)
fz: 0.05-0.10mm
Coolant: flood, high concentration
Tip: Work hardening is enemy #1! Never rub (minimum fz!). M-Grade insert, always a sharp tool.
Titanium (Ti-6Al-4V)
Vc: 30-60 (carbide)
fz: 0.04-0.08mm
Coolant: flood mandatory!
Tip: Low thermal conductivity = heat stays in the tool. Low Vc, deep ap (not shallow!), Trochoidal recommended.
Inconel/Superalloy
Vc: 15-30 (carbide)
fz: 0.03-0.06mm
Coolant: high-pressure flood
Tip: Work hardening is worse than SS! Trochoidal is almost mandatory. AlCrN/ceramic. Steady load — avoid rubbing.
Gray Cast Iron
Vc: 80-150 (carbide)
fz: 0.10-0.20mm
Coolant: dry preferred!
Tip: Graphite self-lubricates — coolant can cause thermal shock and cracking. Dust — good extraction needed.
Copper/Bronze/Brass
Vc: 150-300 (carbide)
fz: 0.10-0.20mm
Coolant: usually not needed
Tip: Pure copper is gummy — sharp tool + high Vc. Free-machining brass (leaded) — excellent machinability.
Hardened Tool Steel (D2/H13)
Vc: 40-80 (carbide)/80-200 (CBN)
fz: 0.05-0.15mm
Coolant: process-dependent
Tip: Above HRC45 — switch to Hard Turning with CBN instead of grinding. Small ap, machine rigidity is critical.
⚙️ Parameter Selection by Process
For a full explanation of how each process works (turning/milling/drilling/grinding) — see the "Manufacturing Technologies" tab. Here: parameter selection only.
Turning
Roughing: ap=2-5mm, fn=0.2-0.4
Finishing: ap=0.2-0.5mm, fn=0.05-0.15
Tip: A Wiper Insert for finishing = double the feed at the same Ra. Minimum finish depth: above 0.1mm (below = rubbing).
Milling
Slotting: ae=100%D
Shoulder: ae=30-50%D
Trochoidal: ae=5-15%D, ap=1-3×D
Tip: Climb milling always on CNC. Trochoidal for SS/Ti/Inconel = 30-300% higher MRR.
Drilling
L/D≤4: normal drilling
L/D 4-10: Peck Drilling (G83)
L/D>10: Gun Drill + internal coolant
Tip: A spot drill for accurate positioning before a long drill. Carbide+TSC drill = 3-5× the speed of HSS.
Grinding
Surface: 0.005-0.02mm/pass
Cylindrical: wheel Vc 25-35 m/s
Tip: Grinding burn = hidden damage! Frequent dressing, adequate coolant, not too aggressive an infeed.
Tapping
Rigid Tapping: n=const, F=P×n
Form Tap: a larger drill (separate table)
Tip: 75% thread depth is almost always enough. Spiral Point for through holes, Spiral Flute for blind holes.
Thread Milling
Vc like regular milling, one tool for every diameter
Tip: Mandatory for hard materials and large holes. Diameter control via Offset — corrects wear!
🔍 Machining Troubleshooting — by Symptom
Visual identification of the problem is the core skill in machining diagnostics — that's why visual reference links are included here.
Chip Not Breaking / Stringing
Cause: unsuitable chip breaker, fz too low, Vc not matched to material.
Solution: increase fz, check insert geometry, smaller Rε.
▶ YouTube
BUE — Built-Up Edge
Cause: Vc too low, gummy material (Al/soft SS), insufficient coolant.
Solution: increase Vc, DLC/TiAlN coating, adequate coolant, a sharper tool.
▶ YouTube
Chatter — Vibration and Wavy Finish
Cause: natural frequency, large overhang, low holding rigidity.
Solution: change RPM (Stability Lobes!), reduce ap, a shorter tool, good clamping.
▶ YouTube
Fast Tool Wear (large VB)
Cause: Vc too high, unsuitable coating, insufficient coolant.
Solution: reduce Vc per Taylor, upgrade coating, check coolant flow to the cutting zone.
▶ YouTube
Poor Surface Finish (high Ra)
Cause: fn/fz too high, small Rε, a worn tool, chatter.
Solution: reduce feed, a Wiper Insert, check tool wear, ensure holding rigidity.
Sudden Tool Breakage
Cause: overload, sudden entry (no ramping), inconsistent material/inclusions.
Solution: ramping/helical entry, check raw material, reduce ap/fz on first entry.
Overheating / Discoloration on the Part
Cause: coolant not reaching the zone, high Vc, low fz (rubbing).
Solution: through-tool coolant, check nozzle direction, increase fz to prevent rubbing.
Dimensional Drift
Cause: accumulated tool wear, machine/spindle heating, outdated tool offset.
Solution: frequent probing, wear compensation via offset, machine warm-up before precise work.
