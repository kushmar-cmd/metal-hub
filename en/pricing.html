📦 Step 1 — Raw Material
How much material do you need to buy to produce one part? The finished part's weight is multiplied by the waste factor (BTF) — because more material always goes in than remains in the finished part.
Weigh the part or calculate from CAD. Not the raw stock — the finished part!
How many kg of raw stock you need to buy for every kg of finished part. BTF 1.3 = 30% waste.
Approximate price per kg of bar/plate at medium quantity in Israel.
—
⚙️ Step 2 — Machining
How long it takes to set up the machine (Setup) and how long the machine runs on each part. Setup is divided across the whole quantity — the more parts, the lower the setup cost per part.
Machine setup time: fixturing, tool setting, zero setting, first-article run. If there are 2 OPs (setups) — add both together.
How many minutes the machine runs on one part, including all operations (OPs). A simple pin = 2-5 minutes. A complex part = 60-120 minutes.
Machine hour rate including: operator, machine depreciation, electricity, cutting tools, coolant. A more advanced machine = a higher rate.
The larger the quantity, the lower the setup cost per part. 1 part = expensive. 1000 parts = cheap.
—
🎨 Step 3 — Finish, Inspection & Extras
Additional costs after machining: coating (anodize, zinc...), heat treatment (hardening), quality inspection, and cleaning/finishing. Not every part needs everything — select only what's relevant.
A protective coating against corrosion/wear. Price per part at medium series.
Hardening, softening, or improving mechanical properties. Required for steels that need hardness.
Measurement, report, CMM. A simple part = basic inspection. Aerospace = full FAI + CMM.
Removing burrs, edge breaking, cleaning. Almost every part requires at least manual deburring.
—
💰 Step 4 — Summary & Price
Overhead = costs not directly tied to the part: rent, electricity, insurance, management, coolant. Typically 25-40%.
Margin = what's left for the shop after all expenses. 15-25% is typical in Israel.
A small shop ~25%. Medium ~30%. Large/aerospace ~35-45%.
Gross margin on the price. 15% = competitive. 20% = reasonable. 25%+ = premium/aerospace.

📄 Quotation — Structure and Method
Professional Quote Structure
1. HeaderCompany name, logo, date, quote number, validity
2. Customer DetailsName, contact person, email, RFQ Reference
3. Technical DescriptionP/N, Rev, material, quantity, attached drawing
4. Unit PriceBreakdown: material + machining + finish + inspection
5. NRENon-Recurring: fixture, programming, special tooling
6. Lead TimeBusiness days! FAI + series
7. MOQMinimum quantity + price breaks
8. Payment TermsNet 30/60, deposit?, currency
9. ExclusionsWhat's not included! (coating? NDT? packaging? cert?)
10. SignatureQuote validity (30/60 days), T&C
▸ Price Break: 10/50/100/500 units — with a price reduction
▸ Validity: 30 days standard. Special materials = 14 days
▸ NRE: always separate! The customer pays it once

📊 Pricing Formulas & Metrics
Basic Formulas
Material Cost= finished weight × BTF × ₪/kg
Machining Cost= (Setup/quantity + machine time) × rate
Direct Cost= material + machining + finish + inspection
Full Cost= direct cost × (1 + overhead%)
Selling Price= full cost × (1 + margin%)
NRE= fixture + programming + tooling + first FAI
Break-Even= NRE / (price − variable cost)
Performance Metrics — KPIs
OEE= Availability × Performance × Quality
OEE World Class≥ 85%
Scrap Rate= scrap / production quantity. Target: < 2%
On-Time Delivery≥ 95%. < 90% = a problem!
Setup Ratio= setup time / production time. Target: < 15%
Spindle UtilizationSpindle hours / available hours. Target: > 60%
Cost/Part Trend↓ with the learning curve — Wright's Law: C_n = C₁ × n^b
▸ Learning Curve: 80% LC = every quantity doubling ← ↓20% cost
▸ Wright's Law: b = log(LC)/log(2) = log(0.8)/log(2) = −0.322

Rent / DepreciationBuilding, machines, equipment
Electricity + WaterCNC = 15–40 kW × ₪0.5/kWh
Coolant + OilsReplaced every 3–6 months
