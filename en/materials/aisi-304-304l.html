304 / 304L Stainless Steel

An austenitic Cr-Ni stainless steel (18/8), the most common stainless steel in the world

Typical values for the temper in the title

304 is an austenitic stainless steel with about 18% chromium and 8% nickel, which is why it is also called "18/8". The chromium forms a thin, self-healing oxide layer on the surface, which is what protects it from corrosion. Annealed, its tensile strength is 515–620 MPa, and it is non-magnetic.

304L is the same alloy with low carbon (max 0.030%). The low carbon prevents chromium carbides forming at grain boundaries during welding, so it is preferred for welded structures. Today most material is sold dual-certified as 304/304L.

Conditions and heat treatment

Austenitic stainless is not hardened by heat treatment. The condition is set by cold work and annealing.

ConditionPropertiesWhen to choose it
Annealedthe normal condition: soft, ductile, most corrosion resistantmost applications
Cold worked (¼H to full hard)much higher strength, slightly magneticstrong springs, sheet and strip
Not hardenable by heat treatment—austenitic stainless strengthens only by cold work

Applications

  • Food and beverage equipment
  • Tanks, piping and light chemical equipment
  • Kitchenware and sinks
  • Architecture and interior cladding
  • Bolts and nuts (A2 per ISO 3506)

Fabrication

Welding

Welds excellently with TIG and MIG, usually with ER308L filler. For welded structures 304L is preferred, to avoid intergranular corrosion next to the weld.

Machining

Relatively hard to machine: it is gummy and work hardens. Use sharp tools, a steady feed that does not dwell in the cut, and good cooling. For high-volume machining, 303 is better.

Corrosion

Excellent in the atmosphere and in fresh water. With chlorides (seawater, salt, chlorinated cleaners), pitting may appear, and above about 60 °C also stress corrosion cracking. For such environments, 316 is better.

Common mistakes

Using 304 in seawater or a pool

Chlorides cause pitting. For such an environment 316, and in harsh conditions duplex.

Welding standard 304 in thick sections

Weld heat forms carbides next to the weld, and the zone becomes corrosion-sensitive. For welded structures, 304L.

Assuming stainless never rusts

After welding, grinding or contact with ordinary steel tools, clean and passivate so the protective layer returns.
