Typical values for the temper in the title

6016 is an Al-Si-Mg alloy with excess silicon, and the standard European alloy for outer car panels: hoods, doors and roofs. It is supplied in soft T4 (190–220 MPa tensile) and forms well.

Its final strength comes in the paint oven, which acts as aging. The excess silicon strengthens, but slightly raises the sensitivity to intergranular corrosion, so it is tested to automotive standards.

Common tempers

Automotive sheet is supplied in T4 and gains most of its strength only in the paint oven (bake hardening).

Applications

  • Hoods
  • Doors and outer panels
  • Roofs and trunk lids
  • Visible sheet parts of vehicles
  • Panels that are hemmed

Fabrication

Forming

Forms well in T4, including hemming of outer panels.

Joining

Welds by spot, MIG and laser welding, and in vehicles is also joined by adhesive bonding and rivets.

Finishing

Suited to automotive paint lines; the final strength comes in the paint oven.

Common mistakes

Designing to T4 strength

The final strength comes only after painting. Design uses the values after the paint cycle.

Storing T4 sheet for a long time

Natural aging continues and makes forming harder. Follow the producer's storage times.

Choosing 5182 for an outer panel

5182 forms Lüders lines. For a visible panel, 6016.
