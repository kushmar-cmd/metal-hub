7075-T6 Aluminum

A 7000-series Al-Zn-Mg-Cu alloy, one of the strongest in common use

Typical values, T6 temper

7075 is an aluminum alloy whose main alloying element is zinc (about 5.6%), with magnesium and copper. Alcoa introduced it in 1943, and it has been the workhorse alloy of the aerospace industry ever since. In the T6 temper (artificially aged) it reaches a typical tensile strength of 572 MPa, about 1.8 times that of 6061-T6, at the density of aluminum.

The price of that strength: fusion welding is practically ruled out, general corrosion resistance is moderate, and the T6 temper is susceptible to stress corrosion cracking (SCC). Choosing 7075 is therefore mostly about choosing the right temper and the right protection.

Applications

  • Highly loaded aerospace structures: fittings, frames, wing and fuselage parts
  • Defense assemblies where strength-to-weight is critical
  • Plate for molds, fixtures and jigs
  • Sports and racing equipment
  • Highly stressed mechanical parts where weight matters

Fabrication

Machining

Machines well. Its relatively high hardness breaks the chip and gives a good finish, usually better than the softer 6061, which tends to smear. For precise or thin parts, order T651 or T7351 plate (stress relieved by stretching) to limit distortion, and work in stages: rough, pause, finish. Use sharp, positive-rake tools and good cooling.

Welding

Fusion welding (TIG, MIG) is not recommended: the alloy is prone to hot cracking, and the heat-affected zone loses much of its strength. Join with rivets, bolts or adhesives. Friction stir welding (FSW) is possible and used.

Corrosion and protection

General corrosion resistance is lower than that of 6061 because of the copper. In humid or marine environments it is usually protected: anodizing (including hard anodizing, Type III), conversion coating and paint, or Alclad sheet (clad with purer aluminum). Under sustained tensile load, mainly in the short-transverse direction of thick plate, T6 is susceptible to stress corrosion cracking; there, T73 or T7351 is the choice.

Common mistakes

Welding 7075 like 6061

Fusion welding creates cracks and a weakened zone. If the part must be welded, the material is probably wrong.

T6 in a thick part under sustained load

In a humid environment this invites stress corrosion cracking. Choose T73 or T7351.

Replacing 6061 with 7075 to reduce deflection

The modulus is almost the same (71.7 vs 68.9 GPa). Strength goes up, stiffness does not. To reduce deflection, change the geometry.

Machining a thin part from ordinary T6 plate

Residual stresses distort it once it comes off the machine. Order T651 or T7351.
