2618-T651 Aluminum

A 2000-series Al-Cu-Mg-Ni-Fe alloy that keeps its strength when hot

Typical values for the temper in the title

2618 differs from the rest of the 2000 series: besides copper and magnesium it contains about 1% nickel and about 1% iron. These elements form stable particles that do not dissolve when hot, so it keeps its strength at around 200 °C and above, far beyond what 2024 holds.

It was chosen for the skin and structure of Concorde, which heated up in supersonic flight, and today it is the classic alloy for forged racing pistons and jet engine parts. In T651 its tensile strength is about 440 MPa.

Applications

  • Forged pistons for racing and boosted engines
  • Jet engine and compressor parts
  • Hot structures, such as the skin of Concorde
  • Forged parts for elevated temperature
  • Turbocharger compressor wheels

Fabrication

Elevated temperature

Keeps strength and hardness over time when hot. That is the only reason to choose it, and it is strong at it.

Pistons

2618 pistons are more ductile than cast or 4032 pistons, so they survive detonation and extreme load better. But they expand more when hot and need more cylinder clearance.

Welding and corrosion

Fusion welding is not recommended. Corrosion resistance is low, as in the other copper alloys.

Common mistakes

Fitting a 2618 piston at a cast-piston clearance

2618 expands more. Without proper clearance the piston may seize.

Choosing 2024 for a part working above 150 °C

2024 loses strength in prolonged heat. For heat, 2618.

Welding 2618

Fusion welding is not recommended.
