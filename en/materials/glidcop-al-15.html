Typical values for the temper in the title

GlidCop is copper with tiny aluminum oxide (Al2O3) particles dispersed in it. In AL-15 the number indicates about 0.15% aluminum, converted to oxide. The particles are stable even at high temperatures, so unlike age-hardened copper alloys, GlidCop keeps its strength even after brazing or long heat exposure.

Its conductivity stays high (close to pure copper). It is used for resistance welding electrodes, particle accelerator and fusion reactor components, and contacts and conductors that run hot.

Tempers and heat treatment

GlidCop is not heat-treatable. Strength comes from the particles and cold work, and is retained on heating.

ConditionPropertiesWhen to choose it
Cold workedhigh strengthmost uses
After brazing or heatingstrength largely retainedunlike plain copper and age-hardened alloys

Applications

  • Resistance welding electrodes
  • Particle accelerator components
  • Fusion reactor components
  • Hot contacts and conductors
  • Brazed parts needing strength

Fabrication

Joining

Brazed without losing strength. Fusion welding can cause the particles to agglomerate and weaken the weld; electron-beam welding or brazing is common.

Machining

Similar to copper, slightly better.

Conductivity

High, close to pure copper.

Common mistakes

Using plain copper in a brazed part that needs strength

The copper softens. GlidCop keeps its strength.

Fusion welding it conventionally

The weld may weaken.

Choosing it when heat stability is not needed

It is expensive. For cold use there are cheaper alternatives.
