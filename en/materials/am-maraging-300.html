Typical values for the temper in the title

Maraging 300 (1.2709) is one of the most common steel powders in printing. It suits printing because its carbon is very low, so it does not crack from fast cooling. As printed it is relatively soft and easy to machine, and after a simple aging it reaches very high strength and hardness.

Per the EOS (MS1) data sheet: as built Rm about 1200 MPa and about 11% elongation; after aging at 490 °C for 6 hours Rm about 2080 MPa and 50–56 HRC. Main use: injection molding and die-casting tools with conformal cooling channels, and high-strength parts.

Conditions after printing

Fabrication

Surface and fatigue

The surface of a printed part is rough and has small defects. This roughness strongly reduces fatigue resistance, so critical surfaces are machined or polished.

Porosity and HIP

Small voids may remain in a printed part (lack of fusion or gas). Hot isostatic pressing (HIP) closes most internal voids and greatly improves fatigue resistance.

Anisotropy

Properties may differ in the build direction (Z) and in the plane (XY). The build orientation of a critical part is set in advance and checked with test bars from the same build.

Powder safety

Fine metal powder can ignite and explode, and is harmful to breathe. Follow the producer instructions, with grounding, inert atmosphere and respiratory protection.

Common mistakes

Using wrought elongation values

Per EOS, after aging elongation is only about 2%, much lower than wrought maraging.

Machining after aging

The material is very hard. Machine before aging.

Looking for ASTM A579

That is a forgings standard. For the printed material, refer to the producer data sheet and to 1.2709.
